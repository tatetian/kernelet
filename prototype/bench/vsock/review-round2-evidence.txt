Remaining nonblocking observation

E8 [P3, measurement-order limitation]
Locations: run-vsock.c:598-603; proposal:275,302; results.txt methodology.
API runs always follow the alternating pipe/klet pair. This is disclosed and
three repetitions support descriptive medians, but the small API/tenant gap
can still include ordering or drift effects. Before making a causal performance
claim, interleave or rotate all three transports and increase repetitions.
No causal or statistical-significance claim is currently made, so this does
not block the proposal or require another experiment for the requested scope.

Overall assessment
The proposal addresses the whole socket facility: authorization, listener and
connected endpoints, pending setup, ordered partial transfer, notification,
backpressure, shutdown, death, independent shared storage and accounting.
The retained experiment uses real host and tenant userspace paths and two
independent sandboxes. Correctness payloads force wraparound; controlled
blocking and peer-death tests supplement ordinary echo. The new source traces
make the architectural comparison assessable without treating virtual queues,
VM exits, worker wakeups and application copies as interchangeable costs.

The performance conclusions are proportional to the experiment: approximately
74%, 56% and 49% of pipe throughput for tenant end-to-end echo, with higher CPU
cost; API access narrows but does not explain the entire gap. Bulk transfers
do not prove small-transfer performance, scalable locking, production Linux
socket compatibility, or faster interrupt delivery. These remain explicit
limitations/planned work rather than hidden failures. The writing follows the
proposal structure and local background-box style and distinguishes planned
mechanisms from implementation evidence and later acceptance.
